# Scope

## Sole event scope

This repository analyses only media coverage connected to the **NEET 2026 protests**. A media item is eligible when its principal subject—or a precisely timestamped, separable portion—concerns the protests, their stated demands, participant experiences, institutional or political responses, policing, litigation, public impact, or a disputed claim about those events.

A matching keyword alone is not sufficient. General examination preparation, admissions, counselling, unrelated education reporting, and unrelated political coverage are excluded.

## Time window

The rolling collection begins **1 July 2026** and is currently open-ended. Each generated report records an inclusive report-through date (the current date by default), so later runs can continue beyond today without rewriting historical provenance. Evidence published before the start or after the chosen report-through date is rejected. Closing or changing the window requires a documented methodology and manifest version change.

## Eligible media and actors

Eligible sources include reports, debates, interviews, verified news uploads, and their headlines, thumbnails, descriptions, and tickers. Mixed streams are eligible only at identifiable timestamps and use Tier D when identity or attribution remains unresolved.

Researchers must be able to code every relevant actor without forcing a two-sided model:

- protest organisers;
- students and candidates;
- parents;
- government representatives;
- education and examination authorities;
- police;
- ruling and opposition political parties;
- courts and public institutions;
- anchors, correspondents, guests, and experts; and
- another actor only when directly relevant to the event.

## Exclusions and safeguards

Exclude unverifiable reposts, anonymous claims without a verifiable source, private personal data, face recognition or identity inference for ordinary participants, and complete third-party media or transcripts. Do not infer political alignment from identity. Inclusion and annotation never imply endorsement, condemnation, truth, or falsity.

The same sampling, attribution, stance, evidence, and review standards apply to all outlets, speakers, sources, and target actors.
